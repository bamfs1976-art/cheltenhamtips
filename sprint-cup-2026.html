⚙️ Engine rules in force at Haydock

This is the first meeting under the rules Ebor rewrote. Three of them changed how a card gets built:

The Lucky 15 no longer runs by default. It finished Ebor at 0 winners from 15 running legs, losing £29.50 of a £30 outlay while the 72 singles finished within £2.33 of level. Its lifetime record is −82.1% (−£41.85 on £51 across eleven days). It is now offered only when asked for, with that record quoted.

The NB is not the weaker slot. Ebor ran NB 8 winners from 28, NAP 3 from 28, LONG 0 from 16. Season-wide the NB strikes at 23% against the NAP’s 18%. The two carry the identical 50p each-way stake and are treated as equally weighted.

Crossover is information, never a promotion. Ebor Day 3 ran three short-priced consensus horses in one afternoon — Caballo De Mar, Marco Polo and Oklahoma — and all three were beaten favourites, every one named on the card before the off. A 3x-or-more pick under 3/1 does not get the NAP.
Also standing: the LONG slot is conditional — 5+ places, 8/1 or bigger, and a concrete signal, or the race runs two picks only. A GoingStick spread under about half a point is noise, not a draw bias (rule 17, which held on all three days it was tested at York). And each-way place terms come from the bookmaker and are re-checked at the off, because withdrawals drop the band after a race has been priced — that fired on three consecutive days at Ebor.

Haydock Park — the course

Left-handed, flat, galloping — and a genuine test on soft ground. Haydock is a wide, level oval of about 1m5f with a long home straight of roughly four and a half furlongs. It is a fair, stamina-favouring track that rewards a horse who can sustain a gallop rather than one who quickens briefly.

The sprint course is straight and it is stiff. The Sprint Cup is run over a straight 6f that rises to the line. Haydock sprints are not won by pure speed alone — the last furlong finds out horses who have gone too fast too early, and the track has a long history of producing soft-ground sprint winners that quicker surfaces would never have suited.

Draw: on the straight course the favoured side moves with the ground and the rail, and in big sprint fields the pack often splits. That makes it a read-the-strips race, not a rule-of-thumb race. Rule 17 applies in full: a GoingStick spread under about half a point is noise, and the readings get re-checked at the off rather than carried from the morning.

Going: Haydock sits on heavy clay and drains slowly. Soft or heavy ground in early September is entirely possible and it changes the meeting completely — check the forecast on the morning of each card rather than carrying the going forward from the previous day.

📋 What turns this preview into a card

1 · A Racing Post racecard, pasted. One paste per day carries everything the gate and the display contract need — going with GoingStick strips, per-race field size and stalls position, and per runner the cloth number, draw, jockey, trainer strike rate, odds, OR/TS/RPR, form figures and CD flags. It is the primary route (CLAUDE.md §12) and the one to ask for by name.

2 · Each-way terms off the bookmaker. Never off the racecard — its penalty values are racecourse prize money, not places. The Sprint Cup and the Old Borough Cup are the two races here most likely to carry a one-off place special above the standard ladder.

3 · External columns, once they publish. Mullington, Nick Luck, Spotlight, Raceolly, Grimshaw and the HRN big-priced lists get overlaid as badges, with any 2+ source horse we do not hold called out as dissent before the off.
