🎯 The LONG slot — forecast the one way §9 allows, and bounded in one direction only

The slot is handicap-only, and that is arithmetic rather than judgement. Scanning 1 to 60 runners through scripts/lib/place-terms.mjs, a non-handicap pays at most three places at any field size, while the LONG’s place test needs five, or four in a field of sixteen or more. So a non-handicap can never open the slot, whatever turns up on the day. That is why a shape forecast works where a size forecast does not.

🏆 Cheltenham — left-handed, undulating, uphill finish

Left-handed and relentlessly undulating, with the stiffest finish in British jumps racing: a punishing uphill run-in of about a furlong and a half that is the single most decisive feature of the track. Two circuits — the Old Course (sharper, used in November and December) and the New Course (stiffer, used at the Festival) — so November and December form is a guide to March but not a transcript of it. No draw. Stamina and jumping outweigh speed; a horse that travels well and stops on the hill is the classic Cheltenham trap. Going is the primary factor and midwinter ground here moves fast — check it on the morning of every card and again at the off.

🥋 The code changes here — what switches off, and what switches on

This is the first jumps card of the season. §8 governs rather than the flat rules, and the differences are not cosmetic:

The draw badge comes off every pick. Rule 3 makes the draw mandatory on flat racing; over jumps there is no draw at all. The gate already knows this — gate-check.mjs computes its missing-draw failure only when the race reads as flat, so a jumps card does not fail on a leg that does not exist. Verified by replaying a three-race Cheltenham fixture through the real gate: it printed 3 races · 0 pass · 3 NO BET, every one on “no declared runners” and not one on a draw, and “LONG slot: not assessed” beside each — which is the 9 September fix doing its job on a code it had never been run against.
Going becomes the primary factor, not a tiebreak. Rules 11 and 12 bind hardest here: midwinter Cheltenham ground moves fast, and §9 says to read it on the morning of every card and again at the off. Rule 17’s half-point GoingStick test still applies to the reading itself, but there is no draw for a strip differential to move.
Trainer and jockey combinations at the course carry real weight, where on the flat they are a supporting angle. So does proven stamina on the ground. Class is no longer the primary factor the way it is in a flat Group race.
And the Old Course is not the New Course. November and December run on the Old Course (sharper); the Festival runs on the New Course (stiffer). A Paddy Power Gold Cup running is a Cheltenham form line, not a Festival transcript — name the course when citing it.
