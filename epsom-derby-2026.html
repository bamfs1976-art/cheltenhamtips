Epsom Downs · the camber, the Tattenham descent and the run-in

Left-handed · undulating · uniquely demanding. A 1m4f U-shape with no other British track like it. An early uphill climb to the highest point, a sharp downhill swing past Tattenham Corner, a pronounced lateral camber on the back straight that throws unbalanced horses across the field, and a short ~3½f run-in. Horses who race straight, balanced and tactically beat gallopers who lean or pull.

Draw — today's card. In the round-course handicaps (1m1f, 1m2f) and the Oaks (1m4f), low stalls save ground round Tattenham Corner and are the prime angle — reflected in nearly every engine selection above. In the 5f "Dash" sprint, the bias is more pace-dependent: high/stands'-side groups can hold up on the descent, so a high draw isn't the negative it is at 7f+. Always check the going first.

Key trainer angles. Aidan O'Brien dominates the Classics — Coolmore runners with Ryan Moore demand respect, the second string under Lordan / Whelan often beats the market. J. & T. Gosden, Ralph Beckett (multiple Oaks) and Andrew Balding are the home Classic/Listed forces; Balding / Oisin Murphy is the combo to follow across the handicaps (Respond, Dance In The Storm, Stellar Sunrise, Mister Winston all theirs).

Key jockey angles. Ryan Moore, Oisin Murphy, William Buick, Colin Keane and Tom Marquand — the Tattenham Corner positioning move separates the great Epsom rides. A Moore booking on an outsider (e.g. Sterling Knight R6) is always a clue.
