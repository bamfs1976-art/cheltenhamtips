⚙️ Engine rules in force at Ebor

Ebor is the first full festival built under the rules the Goodwood review produced. What changes versus every card before it:

No declarations, no bet. Every race passes the pre-flight gate — declared final field, field size, settling each-way terms and a draw for every runner — or it prints NO BET. A skipped race costs nothing; an unverified race costs the full stake.
The LONG slot is conditional, not automatic. It runs only when the race pays 5+ places (or 4+ in a field of 16 or more), the price is 8/1 or bigger, and there is a concrete signal — course/distance form, a significant jockey booking, or money coming. Otherwise the race runs two picks. At 7% strike rate and 0.47 pts per pick the LONG has been the engine's structural leak.
NAP and NB are equally weighted. Across 11 festivals the NB beats the NAP on both strike rate and points per pick. The NAP label is reserved for a genuine standout; if a card hasn't got one, the card says so.
Crossover is information, never a promotion trigger. No pick moves up a slot or into the Lucky 15 on source count. A 3x+ horse under 3/1 does not get the NAP — it runs as the NB, and the NAP goes to a pick the consensus does not share. Nine multi-source picks ran at Goodwood and not one won.
Market move is recorded on every pick. Full price trail, marked STEAM or DRIFT inline. It is the one mechanical, checkable edge the season produced — but it is weighted, not absolute: at Goodwood a steamer was the beaten favourite while a drifter we dismissed ran second.
Place terms are re-checked at the off. Enhanced places are conditional on runner counts. Any race within two runners of a place-band boundary is flagged on the card and its LONG treated as provisional. The settlement panel states the place count that actually applied.
Dissent is surfaced. Any horse carrying two or more external sources that we do not hold gets called out prominently before the off.

York (Knavesmire) — the course

Left-handed, flat, wide and galloping. York is one of the fairest big tracks in Britain — a broad, level circuit of about two miles with sweeping turns and a long home straight of roughly four and a half furlongs. There are no camber tricks and no tight bends to get squeezed on. Horses get every chance to build momentum, which is why the Knavesmire tends to reward the best horse in the race rather than the best-positioned one.

Draw & pace. On the round course the draw is close to immaterial — there is time and room to recover from a wide stall. It is the straight course (5f and 6f, and the sprint handicaps) where the draw earns its analysis: big fields split into groups, and which side of the track holds the advantage moves with the ground and the rail position. Soft ground has historically favoured the higher numbers on the stands' side. That is a pattern, not a law, so live draw data beats a rule of thumb every time — and per the flat rules the draw goes on every pick.

Going. The Knavesmire drains reasonably but sits on low-lying ground beside the Ouse, and August meetings here have been lost or softened by rain before. Going is the single input most likely to move between now and Wednesday — it gets checked against the forecast on the morning of each card, not assumed from the week before.

Angles. A quality track for quality yards: Aidan O'Brien, John & Thady Gosden and Charlie Appleby all have strong records here, and the northern handicap specialists are the reliable read in the big-field pots.
