⚠ Retraction — a finding this page asserted three times in two days is now false.

This card has said, at four and then five columns deep: “there is still not one 2x agreement on a horse we do not hold… every external pick outside our card is a lone opinion… the dissent rule produces nothing to flag.”

Nick Luck’s column arrived this morning and produced two of them immediately. Seven Nation Army (13:50) is now Spotlight + Luck. King Of Earth (14:25) is now Grimshaw + Luck. Both are horses we do not hold, both at 2x, and both are named below.

The honest reading is that the finding was never a property of the card — it was a property of the sample. Five columns produced no overlap outside our picks; a sixth produced two in five tips. It should have been reported as “none yet” rather than as a result, and the confidence grew with each restatement while the evidence did not. Do not report an absence as a finding until the sample stops growing.
Going — and why there is no draw call on this card. Doncaster is riding Good to Soft, Good in places, GoingStick 6.1, with light rain falling and rail movements of +19yds at 2.25 and 5.20 and +9yds at 3.00. The stick has come up from 6.0 on Monday’s entry card, so the track has if anything quickened slightly despite the rain.

But Doncaster publishes a single GoingStick figure, not the Far Side / Centre / Stands’ Side strips York gives. Rule 17 says a spread under about half a point is noise; here there is no spread to read at all. Four of today’s races are on the straight course (6f111y twice, 7f6y, 1m) with the stalls in the centre, which is exactly where a side bias would matter — and we cannot see one. Every pick carries its draw as rule 3 requires, and not one selection has been moved by it. That is the honest position, and it is the same discipline that cost us Henley On Thames at Ebor when we did move a pick on a 0.2 difference.

That one divergence is what earns his agreement in the other race the right to count as a second opinion.

Spotlight is still one desk. Eight tips, five named writers, one verdict per race — eight opinions from one column, not eight sources. The arithmetic that matters: 29 tips from 6 independent columns (5 mastheads — William Hill carries two), plus 5 aggregator rows counted as zero.

Doncaster — the course

Left-handed, flat and galloping, and one of the fairest tracks in Britain. Doncaster is a wide, level, pear-shaped circuit of about 1m7½f with a long home straight of roughly four and a half furlongs. There is also a straight mile, used for the May Hill and the mile nurseries.

It rewards the best horse rather than the best-positioned one. There are no tight bends, no camber tricks and no short run-in, which is why it has staged the St Leger since 1776 — a genuine test of a stayer over 1m6f115y, and the same trip as the Park Hill.

Draw: close to immaterial on the round course. It matters on the straight track, where big fields split into groups and the favoured side moves with the ground and the rail — the Thursday card has three straight-course races with 25, 28 and 17 entered. Rule 17 applies: read the GoingStick strips, require a spread of about half a point before letting it move a pick, and re-read them at the off.

Going: reported Good to Soft, GoingStick 6.0 on the morning of 10 September. Doncaster drains better than Haydock but September rain moves it quickly — check it daily rather than carrying it forward, which is the direct lesson of the Sprint Cup, where the going moved on all three days and Friday's card was priced on the wrong surface.
