This is the exact failure the pre-flight gate exists to catch, and it is worth showing the working rather than just asserting it. Four independent things say entries:

1 · Twelve horses are entered in two races on the same afternoon. A horse cannot run twice in one day. 3 · There is not a single draw anywhere on the card, and many runners have no jockey at all. Rule 3 puts a draw on every flat pick; eight flat races with none of them cannot be priced.

Why this matters more than it looks.

The gate was written after Goodwood 2026, where six picks in three unverified races returned nothing and cost £9 — 11.5% of that festival’s entire turnover. Entry lists there routinely halved: 29→18, 16→8, 22→14, 10→5, 31→24.

Pricing this card would mean naming horses that will not run, in fields that do not exist, with draws invented to fill the gap. A skipped race costs nothing. An unverified race costs the full stake.

One data error worth flagging too: Flying Pirate in the 16:45 is listed at 0st 6lb carrying a 6lb penalty, with a Topspeed of 201 and an RPR of 217. Those figures are impossible — the best RPR on the whole card is 124. Something is corrupt in that row, which is a second reason not to build anything on this paste.

The shape of the meeting is real even if the fields are not. Eight races, two Group 2s — the Park Hill (1m6f115y) over the St Leger trip and the May Hill (1m) for 2yo fillies — plus the £300,000 Weatherbys 2-Y-O Stakes, which at £147,540 to the winner is worth more than either Group 2.

And the LONG slot looks genuinely open here for the first time since Goodwood. On entries, six of the eight races have 16 or more — 25, 28, 17, 16, 17, 16, 21 and 18. The slot needs a 16-runner handicap and Haydock never came close in 22 races. If even half of these hold their numbers, the LONG opens on Thursday. That is the single thing most worth watching at declarations.

Doncaster — the course

Left-handed, flat and galloping, and one of the fairest tracks in Britain. Doncaster is a wide, level, pear-shaped circuit of about 1m7½f with a long home straight of roughly four and a half furlongs. There is also a straight mile, used for the May Hill and the mile nurseries.

It rewards the best horse rather than the best-positioned one. There are no tight bends, no camber tricks and no short run-in, which is why it has staged the St Leger since 1776 — a genuine test of a stayer over 1m6f115y, and the same trip as the Park Hill.

Draw: close to immaterial on the round course. It matters on the straight track, where big fields split into groups and the favoured side moves with the ground and the rail — the Thursday card has three straight-course races with 25, 28 and 17 entered. Rule 17 applies: read the GoingStick strips, require a spread of about half a point before letting it move a pick, and re-read them at the off.

Going: reported Good to Soft, GoingStick 6.0 on the morning of 10 September. Doncaster drains better than Haydock but September rain moves it quickly — check it daily rather than carrying it forward, which is the direct lesson of the Sprint Cup, where the going moved on all three days and Friday's card was priced on the wrong surface.
