🎯 The LONG slot, settled before a runner is named

The slot needs 5+ places paid (or 4+ in a field of 16 or more), a price of 8/1 or bigger, and a concrete signal. On the corrected place bands that reduces to one thing: a handicap of sixteen or more runners. Nothing else clears it.

At this meeting the candidate is the Caspian Caviar Gold Cup. The one handicap on the card likely to carry a field size worth assessing. The Graded races will run small and shut the slot on the place count.

But this is a forecast, and the archive says forecasts of field size are worthless. The St Leger predicted the slot would open in six of eight races off an entry list; declarations cut it to one, and §9 now records that an entry list tells you nothing about field size — which is the only input the place test has. Treat the line above as what to watch for, never as a decision.

🏆 Cheltenham — left-handed, undulating, uphill finish

Left-handed and relentlessly undulating, with the stiffest finish in British jumps racing: a punishing uphill run-in of about a furlong and a half that is the single most decisive feature of the track. Two circuits — the Old Course (sharper, used in November and December) and the New Course (stiffer, used at the Festival) — so November and December form is a guide to March but not a transcript of it. No draw. Stamina and jumping outweigh speed; a horse that travels well and stops on the hill is the classic Cheltenham trap. Going is the primary factor and midwinter ground here moves fast — check it on the morning of every card and again at the off.
