📖 Ayr Racecourse Guide

🏟️ The Circuit

Ayr is a left-handed oval track with a circumference of approximately 1½ miles on the jumps course. The circuit is largely flat with a gentle downhill run approaching the home turn and a slight rise to the finish. The run-in measures 210 yards — shorter than Cheltenham but longer than Aintree.

The Scottish Grand National covers 3 miles 7 furlongs 176 yards with 27 fences over multiple circuits. Ayr's fences are notably straightforward — well-built and fair — with no Aintree-style open ditches or Canal Turn equivalent. The course rewards bold, accurate jumping and genuine staying ability above all else.

🌧️ Going & Ground

Ayrshire springs are notoriously wet. The going at the Scottish Grand National is frequently Good to Soft or Soft. The track drains reasonably well for its coastal location but heavy rain in the week before can produce deep going that suits proven stayers over speedier types.

On soft or heavy ground the race becomes a real test of stamina — the field can string out early and come home in ones and twos. On good ground the race is run at a stronger gallop; jumping becomes a premium and the race can turn into a sprint from the home straight.

🎯 Trainer Records (Scottish Grand National)

Willie Mullins dominates modern renewals — back-to-back wins in 2024 (Macdermott) and 2025 (Captain Cody). He has 5–6 entries in the 2026 race and is the form yard to oppose at your peril.

Paul Nicholls won back-to-back with Vicente in 2016–17. Christian Williams won back-to-back in 2022–23 and has Ask Brewster entered for 2026. Lucinda Russell and Nicky Richards represent the best of the local Northern/Scottish stables.

Trainers to note in 2026: Mullins (Chosen Witness, Olympic Man, Captain Cody, Macdermott, Spanish Harlem), Nicholls (Quebecois, Isaac Des Obeaux), Richards (Famous Bridge), Russell (King Of Answers), Twiston-Davies (The Kniphand).

🔴 Red Rum — The Ayr Legend

In 1974, Red Rum became the only horse in history to win both the Scottish Grand National and the Aintree Grand National in the same season. Trained by Ginger McCain and ridden by Brian Fletcher, Red Rum won the Aintree race in record time before returning to Ayr to complete the double. He remains the greatest National horse of all time — winner at Aintree three times (1973, 1974, 1977) and runner-up twice.
