Ascot · the galloping round, the straight track and the uphill finish

Right-handed · galloping · stiff, testing finish. A roughly triangular 1m6f round course with a long sweeping home bend into a stiff uphill straight (~2½f), plus a separate straight course used for races up to a mile (5f, 6f and the straight mile of the Royal Hunt Cup & Britannia). It is a true galloping track that rewards class, stamina and a horse that stays every yard — the uphill finish finds out the non-stayers and the weak.

Draw & pace — the heritage handicaps. Draw is critical in the big straight-track fields — the Royal Hunt Cup (1m, Wed), Britannia (1m, Thu) and Wokingham (6f, Sat) regularly go to post with 25–30 runners that split into two groups. The favoured side swings with the going and where the pace congregates (stands' rail vs far rail) — we'll call the bias once field sizes, the draw and the watering are confirmed. State the draw on every straight-track selection.

Key trainer angles. Aidan O'Brien (Ballydoyle saves its best milers and stayers for the Royal meeting), Charlie Appleby (Godolphin — exceptional Royal Ascot record), John & Thady Gosden, William Haggas and Andrew Balding are the dominant yards; Wesley Ward and the US raiders target the 2yo and sprint races. Watch the multi-runner stable signals and the first-time-Ascot 2yo debutants.

Key jockey angles. Ryan Moore (the meeting's perennial top rider), William Buick, Colin Keane, Oisin Murphy and Tom Marquand — the bookings to follow. A Moore booking on a Ballydoyle outsider, or Buick on an Appleby 2yo, is always the strongest stable clue.

Going. Typically Good to Good to Firm in June, but summer storms can turn it Soft and reshape every sprint and handicap. The Ascot watering team manage closely. Forecast watch is the key variable all week — confirm the going before the engine's final Nap / NB / Longshot lock in each day.
