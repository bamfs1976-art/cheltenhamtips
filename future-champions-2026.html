📐 Is the RPR column usable today? Eight races, eight answers

The Cambridgeshire settled that the racecard’s RPR is a projection to each race’s par, not an achieved rating — so it has to be calibrated per race before it is allowed to carry an argument. Two diagnostics: ratio = RPR spread ÷ OR spread (low means compressed toward a par), and r(gap, OR) = how strongly the RPR−OR gap just tracks the mark downwards. A gap correlation near −1 means “well handicapped on the figures” is arithmetic that selects the outsider every time.

📐 The residual is not an instrument when the line is flat — and in the Cesarewitch it is flat

The Cesarewitch fits a slope of +0.0016. That is not a weak line, it is a flat one — flatter than the −0.006 in the Cambridgeshire that produced the whole method. And a flat line has a consequence nobody had noticed: the residual degenerates into the raw RPR. With no slope, resid = RPR − mean(RPR), a constant shift. Measured on this race the correlation between residual and raw RPR is 0.999996 and the largest difference is 0.02lb.

So ranking by residual here is ranking by RPR — the exact thing the rule was written to stop. The instrument does not warn you: it returns a tidy ordered list with plausible numbers beside it, and that list is the projection you were trying to escape. The residual is only a reading when the line it is measured against has a slope. Check r(RPR, OR) first; below about 0.3 there is no line, so there is no residual either.

🔧 Settling this meeting found three bugs in the settlement engine, and one of them paid out money that was not won.

1 · The same field meant two different things in two scripts. nr-check.mjs reads a card’s places as what the race was priced at; settle.mjs read it as a settlement override. So the 16:50 nursery — priced at 3 places, run with six horses, settled on the handicap band at 2 — paid out at 3, and rule 16’s “place band moved” line could never fire, because the override had made the two numbers agree by construction. On a fixture that reproduces it, the bug turns a −25% race into +45%, in the direction that flatters us. Places are now always derived from what ran; an advertised special goes in offer with its own minRunners, which is the shape nr-check already tested. One reading of the card across both scripts — the same lesson §10 records about the place bands themselves living in two files.

2 · A non-runner taken at SP was booked as a loss. Two guards sat in the wrong order, so an SP pick that did not run hit the “no card price” branch before the void branch. A void is a property of the race, not the price. It hit Mister Mojito and National Honour on this very meeting — £2.00.

3 · Two of my own derivations disagreed by 0.1pp, and the first fix for it was the wrong way round. A script that re-summed the printed per-pick figures read Friday at −46.9% where settle.mjs said −47.0%: fifteen figures each rounded to the penny, then added, is not the sum rounded once. The fix was to emit a full-precision ledger with --json so nothing reimplements the arithmetic — and that made the number worse while looking more rigorous. Full precision is not the truer figure here. A bookmaker settles each bet and pays it to the penny: the place part of a 7/4 shot at 1/4 odds is £0.71875 and what lands in the account is £0.72. The real return is the sum of penny-rounded returns, which is what the printed lines and the whole season ledger have always been. So the rounding now happens per pick at the point of settlement, the ledger carries it, and the engine, this page and the season ledger all read £18.43 and −40.5%. The lesson is not “use more precision” — it is that the unit of rounding is a domain fact about how a bet is paid, and picking the mathematically tidier answer got it wrong.

All three are pinned by fixtures that fail when the bug is put back: scripts/fixtures/settle-void-regression.json and settle-bands-regression.json. The second carries both halves of the depth test, because the two place ladders only differ above fifteen runners and a test inside one band cannot see the other.

🏆 Newmarket — the Rowley Mile

Right-handed, wide and galloping, with one of the stiffest finishes in Britain. The straight course runs a full mile: it dips about a furlong and a half out and then rises to the line, which finds out anything that committed too early. Draw is close to immaterial in a small field but becomes a first-order factor in the big autumn handicaps, where a wide field splits into groups — the Cesarewitch is the standing example. Rule 17 applies in full: read the GoingStick strips, require about half a point of spread, re-read at the off. Going is free-draining chalk heath, so October ground is usually good or softer only after real rain.
